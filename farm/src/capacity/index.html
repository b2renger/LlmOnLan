Assumptions you can change

The pass rule is the spike's: a level counts when 95 % of first words arrive within the delay and 90 % of replies stream at least that fast. "A class" means everyone presses Enter together; "normal use" means people are out of step. The two-Spark delay was fitted to two community reports, not measured on your boxes. The scenario cards use the first two and the last; each card sets its own reply length.

Where the numbers come from. Measured cells are the spike runs: vLLM 0.30 with NVFP4 checkpoints and an fp8 KV cache, each person's own conversation already in the cache, a 256-token reply, one run per box (RTX PRO 6000 on 2026-10-04, DGX Spark on 2026-10-05/06). Calibrated cells use those models' fitted speeds scaled to the other box. Estimated cells derive bytes read per token from active parameters × bits and the attention cache, scaled by memory bandwidth and compute. Calibrated on the PRO 6000 alone, the method predicted the Spark's measured people within the measured range in 17 of 18 cases (mean error 1 %).

Not modelled. Speculative decoding, sparse attention (long-context figures are pessimistic for GLM-5.3, DeepSeek-V4, MiniMax-M3), engines other than vLLM (llama.cpp served 2–24× fewer people in the spike), and people pausing to read. Prices are French retail with VAT on 2026-10-05/07, card only. Benchmark scores are mostly the vendors' own; the scenario cards rank quality by BenchLM's open-model index, a third-party aggregate.
