Quill (editor)

1 History 2 Releases

Quill is a free and open-source text editor first released in 2011 by a group of students in Lyon. Its stable versions are published by the Quill Foundation, and every stable version since 5.0 has been a long-term support release kept for two years.

History

The first stable version of Quill, 1.0, came out in March 2011 and ran only on Linux. Version 2.0 added Windows support in 2013, and version 3.0 brought the plugin manager that later became the centre of the project. Each stable version was announced on the project mailing list and on its website.

In 2016 the Quill Foundation was created to hold the trademark and pay two full-time maintainers. The foundation also took over the release schedule: one stable version a year, each one a long-term support version, and experimental builds every night for people who want to test the next version.

Quill 4.0, released in 2018, rewrote the rendering engine and doubled the speed of opening large files. Quill 5.0 followed in 2020 with a new settings window and better support for right-to-left languages, and became the first version maintained for two full years after its release.

Quill 6.0 arrived on 1 March 2024 with a new plugin system. Many reviews called it the best version of the editor so far, and it was the stable version most schools installed in 2024 and 2025 before the move to the 7 series.

Quill 7.0 came out in May 2025 and was the first version built with the new interface toolkit. Its stable updates 7.1 and 7.2 followed in 2025 and 2026. The 7 series is the current stable branch of the editor and the one the foundation recommends to every user.

The latest stable version of Quill is 7.2.1, released on 15 September 2026; it fixes a crash when saving to a network folder. The next version, Quill 7.3, is planned for spring 2027 and can be tested in the experimental builds.

Older versions of Quill can still be downloaded from the foundation's archive, but the foundation warns that versions past their end of life receive no security fixes and should not be used on computers connected to a network.

The version numbers follow a simple rule: the first number changes when files written by the new version can no longer be opened by the old one, and the second when new features arrive. A third number marks a release that only fixes bugs, such as 7.2.1 after 7.2.0.

Every stable version of Quill is signed by two maintainers, and its installer is checked by the foundation's build servers before it is published. The release notes of each version list the bugs it fixes and the people who reported them.

Translations of Quill are made by volunteers in more than forty languages. A version is released only when its ten most used languages are complete, which has delayed a stable version twice, in 2019 and in 2022.

Plugins written for one stable version of Quill keep working with the next updates of the same series. Moving to a new series, such as from version 6 to version 7, usually means updating plugins, and the foundation publishes a guide for each such move.
